# Disney Experiences Intelligence Dashboard

**Segment:** Parks, Cruises, Resorts & Consumer Experiences
**Date:** April 16, 2026 | **Period:** Q1 FY2026 + trailing 90-day signals

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Record quarter.** Experiences posted $10.01B in Q1 FY2026 revenue (first-ever $10B+ quarter), with operating income of $3.31B (+6% YoY). The segment generates 38% of Disney's revenue but 71% of operating income — it is the profit engine.
- **Domestic parks strong, international headwinds real.** Domestic parks revenue hit $6.91B (+7% YoY) and international reached $1.75B (+7% YoY), but Disney warned of "modest" forward growth due to declining international visitation to the U.S. (~6% drop in 2025, another ~5% expected in 2026).
- **Cruise fleet doubling is the biggest growth bet.** Disney Adventure (6,700 guests, largest-ever ship) launched from Singapore in March 2026. Fleet grows from 5 to 13 ships by 2031. Pre-launch costs are pressuring near-term margins.
- **Customer sentiment is split.** Guest satisfaction scores remain high per Disney's internal metrics (NPS ~49 at Disneyland), but pricing fatigue is loud — 75% of Harris Poll respondents say Disney is financially out of reach; 24% have taken on debt for a trip.
- **Epic Universe is a credible competitive threat.** Universal's new park is reshaping Orlando trip planning from "5 Disney / 1 Universal" to "4 Disney / 3 Universal," compressing Disney's share of wallet in its core market.

## 3. Top 3 Risks

1. **International visitation decline.** ~23% of WDW guests are international. U.S. policy and tariff headwinds are pushing inbound tourism down, and Disney has already pivoted marketing to domestic audiences — a band-aid, not a fix.
2. **Pricing ceiling approaching.** Revenue growth is increasingly driven by per-guest monetization rather than volume. With 75% of consumers calling Disney "out of reach" and 24% going into debt, the elasticity runway is narrowing. Any consumer spending downturn would hit hard.
3. **Construction-cycle timing vs. competition.** Disney is tearing down and building for 2027-2028 (Villains Land, new attractions) while Epic Universe delivers a finished product today. The gap creates a near-term perception disadvantage.

---

## 4. Top 3 Opportunities

1. **Cruise fleet as a second growth engine.** Doubling from 5 to 13 ships by 2031 opens Asia-Pacific (Singapore launch), diversifies geography, and creates recurring high-margin revenue. Cruise demand indicators remain strong globally.
2. **$60B investment cycle payoff.** World of Frozen (Disneyland Paris, opened March 2026 to strong reception), Avengers Campus expansion, Villains Land, and 1,000+ undeveloped acres represent a multi-year pipeline of demand catalysts.
3. **Per-guest yield optimization.** Lightning Lane, tiered annual passes, premium dining, and dynamic pricing continue to grow revenue per visitor even as attendance softens. Disney is successfully trading volume for margin.

---

## 5. Final Verdict

**Moderately Bullish** on Disney Experiences.

The financial engine is performing: record revenue, strong margins, and rising per-guest spending demonstrate durable pricing power. The cruise expansion into Asia-Pacific and the $60B investment pipeline provide visible multi-year growth vectors. However, this is not an unqualified bull case. Attendance is flat-to-declining, international headwinds are structural (not cyclical), and Epic Universe is the first credible Orlando competitor in decades. The biggest risk is that Disney is optimizing revenue from a shrinking visitor base — a strategy with diminishing returns if consumer sentiment turns. Near-term (6-12 months): expect margin compression from cruise pre-launch costs and construction drag. Medium-term (2-4 years): the investment cycle should unlock meaningful upside as new lands and ships come online.
